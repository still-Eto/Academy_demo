# Decisions

One line per non-obvious choice, and what the parent in the brief gains from it.

## Regulatory

- **Kept IDEC as the licensing agency, verified rather than assumed.** Confirmed the Illinois Department of Early Childhood took over child care licensure from DCFS on July 1, 2026 — a director will know this changed a month ago, and getting it wrong would end the meeting.
- **Kept all six Illinois minimum ratios exactly as briefed.** Verified against 89 Ill. Adm. Code 407.190; every figure was already correct, so nothing needed lowering. The parent gets a comparison she could check herself.
- **Added the Illinois maximum group size to `/safety`.** The brief only asked for ratios, but a director reads both. Every Bridgewood class size clears the state cap, so it is a second free win on the same table.
- **Attributed CCAP to IDEC, not IDHS.** The subsidy program moved on the same date as licensure. A parent on subsidy who is told the wrong department loses a week.
- **Named the training numbers.** Illinois requires 15 clock hours a year and mandated reporter training within 30 days of hire; both are verified, so `/safety` can state ours against the requirement instead of saying "well trained".

## Content and consistency

- **Marisol did not found the school.** The brief says founded 2011 and Marisol has 12 years, which do not reconcile. Invented Carol Nystrom as founder and had Marisol buy the school in 2019. The parent gets a story that adds up; a director gets a page that does not contradict itself.
- **Every number is computed, never typed twice.** Monthly tuition is `weekly × 52 ÷ 12` in code, capacity is the sum of the class sizes, the "7 spaces open" figure counts the openings, and lead-teacher tenure averages the roster. Nothing can drift out of agreement between pages.
- **A room name never appears without its age range.** Enforced by a check in `scripts/verify.mjs` that scans every string literal in the codebase. The one exception is the room's own detail page below its `<h1>`, where the heading already pairs the two — repeating it in every subheading below reads as robotic.
- **Every teacher named in a parent quote is on the staff page, in the room the parent names.** Danielle mentions Jo; Jo teaches in the Sunflower Room. A parent who checks finds it consistent.
- **Wrote the withdrawal answer honestly, including that fees are not refunded.** Nobody does this. It is the most disarming paragraph on the site and costs nothing, because a parent who is leaving was leaving anyway.

## Structure

- **`/book-a-tour` keeps a reduced footer.** The brief says the header carries logo and phone only, and also says the footer appears on every page. Kept the credentials and the address as text with no navigation links, so there is nothing to click away to but the license number is still visible.
- **The five-field rule is five *fields*, not five inputs.** Field five is "preferred tour day (date + morning/afternoon)" exactly as specified: one `<fieldset>` holding a native date control and a two-option choice. Marked with `data-field` so the count is verifiable rather than arguable.
- **The tour form sends nothing (05.11.2026, still-Eto/ertukara#59).** It used to degrade to a real POST: `/api/tour` wrote every request into the Vercel log and, with JavaScript off, redirected back with every entry in the query string. ertukara.com's privacy policy says the demo forms transmit nothing, and the line under the form says nothing is saved, so the endpoint is gone. The form validates in the browser and renders the same confirmation in place; `method="dialog"` makes the button a no-op without JavaScript, and a noscript line says so. A real center's deployment adds its own endpoint, set up for its privacy policy.
- **Everything date-shaped is anchored to UTC noon.** The form renders on the server and again on hydration; computing "tomorrow" in local time would have made the two disagree across a timezone boundary and produced a hydration mismatch.

## Design and build

- **Forest green primary, terracotta accent, warm off-white page.** The accent is reserved for the CTA. Measured: white on terracotta is 6.00:1, the button edge against the page is 5.65:1, and every other text pair clears 4.5:1 — all printed by `npm run verify`.
- **Two status tones, never used alone.** Availability always pairs a tone with a word (`2 spaces`, `Waitlist`) and a distinct icon shape (check, calendar, clock), so it survives colour blindness and a low-brightness screen at 9:40pm.
- **The room switcher is a CSS-only radio group.** No client JavaScript, and it works with JavaScript disabled. Arrow keys move between rooms for free because it is a real radiogroup.
- **The scroll reveal is one observer, not one per section.** `Reveal` is a server component that stamps an attribute; a single client component observes them all. Using it twenty times costs nothing extra.
- **The header receives the room list as a prop.** Importing `content/programs.ts` into a client component would have shipped every rhythm, outcome and photograph to the browser. It gets `{slug, label, ages}` and nothing else.
- **Placeholder art is generated, not hand-drawn, and reads the palette from `site.config.ts`.** Re-run `node scripts/generate-placeholders.mjs` after a re-skin and the art follows the new brand. No external image host is referenced anywhere, so the demo cannot load broken on a prospect's office WiFi.
- **`next/image` uses `unoptimized` for the placeholder SVGs.** The API shape is already correct for the real photography; `PHOTO-SWAP.md` says to drop the flag when real files land.
- **TypeScript 5.9 rather than 7.0.** TypeScript 7 is current, but the ESLint toolchain around Next 16 is not there yet and a demo build should not be the place to find out.
- **The verification scripts refuse to run against a port that is already listening.** A leftover server serving a stale build produces confident, wrong numbers — which happened twice while building this.

## Deleted

- **The twelve-item "On this page" jump nav at the top of `/safety`.** The one section the parent in the brief would not have missed. She does not use a table of contents at 9:40pm — she scrolls or she leaves — and twelve buttons pushed the ratio table, the entire argument of that page, most of a screen further down. It was a convenience for a director skimming, paid for by the parent.

## Fix pass — measured defects found in a real browser

- **The scroll-reveal now fails visible, not invisible.** Twenty-seven elements — every program card, the whole openings table, the safety blocks — were server-rendered at `opacity: 0` and depended on an IntersectionObserver to appear. The hidden state is now gated behind a `js-reveal` class that a tiny inline `<head>` script adds only when JavaScript runs and motion is allowed, so no JavaScript means nothing is ever hidden. The observer reveals anything already on screen at mount rather than waiting for an intersection *change* that a wide viewport, an anchor jump or a restored scroll position never produces, and a 1200ms timer force-reveals everything regardless. The hero and the openings block are out of the reveal entirely — they are what a prospect sees first.
- **The home page title was 101 characters with the brand in it twice.** `title.default` was a full sentence and the `%s · <name>` template appended the brand again. The home route now sets an `absolute` title built from `site.name` and a new `titleTagline` fragment. 51 characters, brand once, no trailing period.
- **On `/book-a-tour`, the reassurance sat below the submit button on mobile.** The desktop two-column layout is right; the mobile stack simply dropped the right-hand column underneath, so the majority of visitors reached the decision point before reading the thing meant to make it feel safe. A compact trust row — Marisol at 48px, her name and the response promise — now sits between the H1 and the form. The three steps and the parent quotes stay below it, and desktop is unchanged.
- **The mobile home page was 14,372px — seventeen phone screens.** Now 9,468px, with all nine sections intact. Mobile section padding to 48px; program cards collapsed to room, ages, ratio-versus-state and weekly rate; the teachers and the parent quotes as swipeable strips; the day timeline putting each time on the same line as what happens; the footer's twelve navigation links in two columns rather than one; and the trailing aside of four intro paragraphs not rendered below `sm`.
- **The whole program card is the link now, not the row at the bottom of it.** A bigger tap target, one target instead of two, and it freed six 48px strips of "Inside the …" chevron.
- **The selected room tab on "A day at Bridgewood" was invisible.** Measured 1.06:1 — near-white label on a white fill, on the live build too. Tailwind's `utilities` layer outranks `components`, so the `bg-surface` utility on the element beat the `:checked ~` rule that was supposed to fill it green while that rule still turned the text white. Both states now live in the same layer. Found by Lighthouse only after the fix pass, not by the brief.
- **The image manifest.** Every image is one typed entry in `content/images.ts` — path, alt, the shot to take, time of day, orientation, intrinsic size, and whether it is the LCP element. Nothing else in the codebase names a file under `/public/img`, `PHOTO-SWAP.md` is generated from it, the placeholder art is generated from it, and `npm run verify:swap` proves a real photo swap costs CLS 0.0000.
- **`content/rooms.ts` exists so the room name and its age range are defined once.** Both the room data and the image manifest build their prose from it, which is why alt text a screen reader hears now carries the age range a sighted visitor reads on the card.

## Photography pass

- **The placeholders no longer say anything.** They used to print the shot brief, the time of day and the pixel dimensions into the artwork — "HERO — PHOTOGRAPH TO COME", in the hero, at 8px on a phone. That is production notes rendered as page content: a director reads it, learns she is looking at a mockup, and re-reads every claim above it as hypothetical. Now every empty slot is a wordless graded block in the brand palette. Zero `<text>` elements across all 30 generated SVGs.
- **Shot briefs moved out of the runtime bundle entirely.** `content/photo-briefs.ts` is imported by two build scripts and by no component, so phrases like "Shot from adult standing height" cannot reach `.next/static` or an SVG's `aria-label` again. Direction for a photographer belongs in the photographer's brief, not in the deploy.
- **Four of the six supplied images were rejected, and the reasons are in the code.** One was Unsplash+ — a paid subscription licence, not the free one, and it was the intended hero. One showed a child in a face mask, which dates the photograph on sight. One carried "GREEN GARDEN PRESCHOOL" across every shirt in frame — a competitor's branding on a page selling this school. One was 1280×720 with legible Korean text on the worksheet. Replacements came from the same free-license sources; three of the five now share one shoot, which is most of why the set does not read as stock.
- **Nothing is graded at runtime.** A CSS filter costs paint time on every view and cannot fix white balance. `npm run photos:build` does it once: crop to the slot's declared ratio, then a per-channel linear that folds a warm bias into a contrast lift, then saturation to 0.92, then a measured exposure match. Mean luminance across the six landed inside a 5.1-point spread of 255 — the first attempt measured exposure before the tone curve and left a 34-point spread, which is not a match.
- **Two Acorn slots and all seventeen staff portraits stay empty on purpose.** Every photograph available shows a toddler or a preschooler. A parent of a six-week-old spots an age mismatch instantly, and it would undermine the 1:3 infant figure printed beside it. Cropping a child's face into a teacher portrait is worse. A clean empty slot beats a mismatched photograph.
- **The mobile hero photograph earns its space, so it went full-bleed.** The alternative was dropping it below the trust line. A small rectangle between the CTA and the openings list would have been worth dropping; a frame of a teacher with five children — the one image that argues the headline — is not.
- **Photographs bypass `next/image`.** The files on disk are already sized and already graded; running them through the optimizer would re-encode an encode. They render as a plain `<picture>` with AVIF, WebP and a JPEG fallback, inside the same aspect-ratio container the blocks use, which is why CLS across the swap is 0.

## Art direction pass

- **The page has a dark ground now, and it is 59.7% of the scroll height.** The diagnosis that started this pass was that four out of four competitor sites open on a full-viewport dark hero with type at enormous scale, and we did the opposite on every axis simultaneously. Ink `#12201A` and Deep `#1E3A2F` are the anchor tones; light and dark alternate down the page instead of one pale field running the whole way. A parent arriving from three other tabs now lands on something that looks like a place rather than a document.
- **The display scale went from 50px to 90.6px at 1568, and body stayed at 17/18px.** The contrast between the two *is* the effect. Raising body to balance it would have deleted the effect and cost the reading experience at the same time.
- **Brass is two values, not one.** `#C9A227` measures 6.96:1 on Ink and 2.31:1 on the page — legible on the dark sections and illegible on the light ones. `#7A5C12` is the same hue at 5.88:1 on the page. One token could not do both jobs, and a single "brass" would have shipped an eyebrow nobody could read on half the site.
- **The openings block stopped being a table.** It is the one thing no competitor has and it was rendered as a spreadsheet export with 11px grey column headers. It is now six cards where the status is the visual event: a room with space sets its number at display scale in brass over a brass rule; a full room is a muted card at lower contrast. The difference is readable from across a room, which is the actual test. The honest copy did not change — two of the six rooms are still full and still say how long the wait is.
- **One rendering of the openings, not two.** It used to ship stacked cards below `md` and a real `<table>` above it. The cards now carry everything the columns did, at a size a phone can read, so there was nothing left for the table to do. This also removed the accidental crutch a verification check was leaning on — see below.
- **The nav overlays the hero, and the no-JavaScript path is why it is shaped the way it is.** A `fixed` transparent bar is only readable while a dark hero is behind it, and without JavaScript nothing can tell it when that stops being true. So the server renders it `absolute` — overlaying the hero and scrolling away with it, which is correct and readable with no script at all — and a mount effect upgrades it to `fixed`. Off the home page nothing changed.
- **Two crops of the hero, from one exposure.** 88vh is a 2.4:1 band on a monitor and a 0.54:1 portrait on a phone. A single 16:9 file `object-fit: cover`-ed into both keeps the teacher on the desktop and throws her out of frame on the phone. `hero` and `hero.mobile` are cropped at build time from the same frame and a `media` attribute picks one, so exactly one is ever fetched — including by the preload.
- **The desktop hero slot is 2560×1200, not 16:9.** The slot ratio has to be near the ratio it is rendered at, because `cover` discards the difference. At 16:9 the crop threw away 226px of height, and what lives in the bottom 226px of that frame is the teacher's hand on the page.
- **Both of the deliberately dark bands got a *lighter* scrim than the hero, not a heavier one.** They sit on `low-key` graded images at mean luma 46 and 37 against 148 for everything else. A 72% scrim on an image already four stops down produces a flat green rectangle with a photograph you have to be told is there.
- **The parent quotes lost their room photographs.** The Acorn Room (6 weeks – 15 months) has no photograph and deliberately never will. On the old light card its wordless block was unobtrusive; on Deep it is a pale rectangle that reads as an image that failed to load, directly under the best quote on the page. The room photographs still appear on the program cards, the six room pages and `/about`.
- **A verification check was measuring the wrong thing, and only passing by accident.** The keyboard check asked whether the Programs menu had closed by looking for the first `a[href="/programs/acorn"]` anywhere on the page. That link belonged to the openings block's mobile-only card list, which was `display:none` at 1440 — so the check was really asking "is there a hidden link somewhere on this page". Making the openings one grid at every width exposed it. It is now scoped to `header` and reads `aria-expanded` alongside, which is the assertion that was always intended.
- **`components/overflow-detector.tsx` was deleted.** A dead duplicate of the `.ts` file next to it, exporting a different symbol. Turbopack resolved the dynamic import to it, so `startOverflowDetector is not a function` threw on every dev page load and stopped the client tree hydrating — which meant the header menus silently did nothing in development. Production was never affected, which is why it survived this long.

## Pre-send review pass

- **A number was compiling hard against the next word, and the source looked correct.** `{staff.length} people` shipped as "17people" in the first sentence of `/staff`, and `{site.director.tenureYears} years here` shipped as "12years here" in the message a parent reads straight after handing over their phone number. When a JSX expression opens a line AND the prose after it wraps onto a second line, SWC drops the leading space of that text node; the single-line version of the same shape is fine, which is why eighteen other instances in this codebase were correct and reading the source told you nothing. Fixed with an explicit `{" "}` at both sites, and `npm run verify:text` now scans every prerendered page and every client chunk for the defect — proven by reintroducing both bugs and watching it fail.
- **Seventeen identical grey silhouettes are worse than no photograph.** All seventeen staff "portraits" were the same 342-byte SVG under different filenames, rendered down a page headed "Everyone who works at Bridgewood". Next to the real photography everywhere else on the site they did not read as a style decision, they read as seventeen images that failed to load. `ManagedAvatar` now renders nothing when the slot holds no photograph, and the page is a list of named people with tenure, credential and a sentence in their own voice — which is a stronger claim than a placeholder face.
- **That change also fixed a latent bug in the swap promise.** `ManagedAvatar` rendered `image.src` unconditionally, so a center that dropped real portraits into the manifest and ran `photos:build` would have kept seeing the silhouette. It now renders the graded AVIF/WebP/JPEG the same way every other photograph on the site does, so the portraits appear the moment they exist and no component has to change.
- **One `<h1>` on `/book-a-tour`, where there were two.** The page carried a mobile heading and a desktop heading, each hidden at the other breakpoint. No visitor ever met it twice — `display: none` keeps it out of the accessibility tree — but it was still two `<h1>` in the document. Splitting the heading into its own grid child fixed it without moving a pixel, and incidentally fixed a smaller bug: the mobile heading sat inside the `booked ?` branch, so submitting the form left the phone with no visible heading at all.
- **The tour form now says what happens to the data, at the point it is asked.** "What do you do with my phone number" is a fair question and nothing on the site answered it. The line follows the deployment: on a demo it says nothing is saved, emailed or passed on, which since 05.11.2026 (#59) is literally true because nothing leaves the browser; on a real center's deployment it states the purpose instead.
- **The demo disclaimer is conditional on the flag that already means "demo".** Two real accrediting bodies are named on the site and both marks link to the real registries, while the license number, address and phone are invented. That is fine behind `noindex` and a `Disallow: /`, and not fine once the URL is pasted into an email. The footer line appears when `NEXT_PUBLIC_NOINDEX` is set and disappears by itself on the real center's site, where every claim above it is true.
- **A rejected field looks rejected.** Only the message under a field changed on a validation failure; the border stayed the same colour as the five fields that were fine. Now keyed off `aria-invalid`, so the visual state and the announced state are the same fact. It has to live in the `utilities` layer — written in `base` first, the inset shadow appeared and the border colour silently did not, because layer order beats specificity and the fields carry Tailwind's `border-line`.
- **The hero eyebrow no longer welds two true facts into a false one.** It read "Licensed since 2011". The school opened in 2011; Illinois licensure only moved to IDEC on 1 July 2026, so a 2011 licence would have carried a DCFS prefix rather than the IDEC number printed in the trust line below it. Now "Since 2011", with the licence stated separately and without a date attached.

## Warming pass

The first art-direction build overshot. On a childcare site it read as heavy — a green-black ground under 60% of the page, and two photographs graded dark and then covered by a heavy scrim, so they were invisible. Four changes, in order of how much each one mattered.

- **The dark tones are warm now, and they stayed green.** `#12201A` → `#24301F` and `#1E3A2F` → `#2A3A26`: both carry more red than blue, which is what warm means in a channel, and both are about two stops lighter. Brown would have been truer to "dark wood" and was rejected — the terracotta CTA is a red-brown, and on a brown ground the one button that has to be found stops separating from what is behind it. Brass on `deep` is the binding constraint at 5.0:1; any further lift and the eyebrow on the location band fails.
- **The low-key grade target went 46 → 75, and the scrims came down with it.** 46 was a mistake made twice: a photograph already four stops down, then covered at 70%. Between the two, the day timeline and the closing band were near-black rectangles with something you had to be told was behind them. The exposure clamp had to widen at both ends — the ceiling to 2.6 so the playground can reach the target instead of pinning against it, the floor to 0.70 so the empty room can come down to it.
- **The scrims were tuned by measurement, not by eye, and the first attempt failed.** Going from 94% to 76% on the hero produced 3.12:1 under the headline, 2.97:1 on the day timeline and 3.38:1 on the closing band. All three are now back up to about 5.1:1 — lighter than they were, dark enough to carry white type over the brightest pixel each photograph actually contains.
- **Two bands were replaced.** The safety band was described in this repo as "an empty room with the easels folded against the wall" — true of the frame, and not true of the 2:1 slice cut out of it, which was folded easels, a tub of oddments, a waste bin and a blank white table. The mid-page break was a good photograph on a cold grey floor between two warm sections, with a legible product sticker on the toy in the corner. The strongest replacement candidate for the safety band was rejected for being a 3D architectural render: on a page whose argument is "come and stand in the room", a room that does not exist is the one thing the photography cannot be.
- **A band's declared ratio has to be near the shape it renders at.** Both bands are the full width of the viewport and about a third of its height — a 5:1 slice on a desktop. Declared at 2:1 and 2.4:1, `object-fit: cover` was throwing away 43% of the height, and what it took was the row of blocks in one and the chairs in the other. Same lesson as the hero, learned again.
- **The staff silhouettes came back, on a component I had not checked.** `ManagedAvatar` was fixed to collapse when a slot holds no photograph; the teacher strip on the home page uses `ManagedImg`, so four grey faces reappeared under "how long they have been here". Fixed the same way. The wordless blocks for ROOMS stay — an abstract colored rectangle where a room photograph will go is a deliberate empty slot, and a face-shaped grey hole where a person will go is not.

## Brightening pass

The warming pass fixed the hue and the crushed photographs and still left the page reading heavy — because the art direction reached for a dark ground every time a section needed to look different from the one above it, and there were six of them. This pass stopped doing that.

- **Sections are separated by three LIGHT grounds now, not by darkness.** The page `bg`, a new warm `sand` (`#F5EEE3`) and the pale `primaryTint`. Same rhythm rule — no two identical sections in a row — none of the weight. The openings block, the day timeline, the parent quotes and the location band all moved off the dark ground; dark went from 55% of the page to 22%, and what is left of it is the hero and the closing band, both photographs a parent looks into rather than fields they read across.
- **The verification check for it is a band now, not a floor.** It asserted "at least 35% dark", which was right for the problem it was written for — a page with no tone darker than its body text — and built to, it produced 60%. It now asserts 15–40%: below the floor there is no anchor and the page is flat again, above the ceiling it is heavy. Moving a threshold to match the work is only honest if you say so, so: I moved it, and this is why.
- **The hero scrim is a LEFT-BIASED ramp, and that is where the brightness came from.** A bottom-up scrim darkens the entire width of the frame to make one column of type legible. The type is not spread across the width — it sits in the left half. Weighting the darkness to where the words are lets the right of the photograph come up to 8%, which is the difference between a hero you read and a hero you look into.
- **The headline measure went 22ch → 17ch for the same reason, and this is the trade worth naming.** White type on a photograph needs about 65% of darkness under it whatever else is true, so every character the headline runs to the right is another column of picture that has to be darkened to carry it. At 22ch the type reached 70% of the frame and took the daylight with it. Narrower type, brighter photograph, and the display size came down from 90.6px to 78px to keep the now-four-line headline inside 88vh.
- **The mid-page band was replaced a second time.** The reclaimed-barn-board frame was warm and sunlit and read farmhouse, not classroom — two sections above a photograph of an actual preschool room. Replaced with a toddler's hand setting a terracotta block on a stack of teal and cream ones: bright, and the block colours are almost exactly this site's palette.
- **The day-timeline photograph was removed rather than left in the manifest.** It was the best low-key frame in the set, which is precisely why it had nowhere to go once that section moved to a light ground — a photograph that only works under white type is homeless on a page that stopped using white type below the fold. Deleting it beats building 1MB of derivatives nothing fetches.
- **The footer went back to forest green.** In `ground` it was a second slab of near-black directly under the closing band, and two of those in a row is most of what made the foot of the page feel heavy.

## Known limitation

- **First-load JS is 156KB gzipped against the brief's 120KB budget.** Measured, not estimated. The Next 16 + React 19 App Router runtime alone is ~144KB gzipped before a single line of application code; the whole site adds about 12KB on top. See `README.md` for the measurement and what it would take to get under 120KB.
